Wu Common Language — Phonological System

The Wu common language is a mixed dialect based on two closely related Wu dialects — mid-generation Suzhounese and old Shanghainese — and incorporates phonological features of various northern Wu dialects. Its phonology and character readings have undergone editorial review and phonetic standardization. The phonological system of the “Wu common language” is grounded in old Shanghainese and mid-generation Suzhounese. The tonal system and tone sandhi are based on mid-generation Suzhounese while remaining compatible with mid-generation Shanghainese variants. Individual character readings are based on Shanghainese and Suzhounese, cross-checked against rhyme books, with colloquial readings given priority. At the same time, innovations shared exclusively by Suzhounese and Shanghainese (i.e., innovations not found in other Wu dialects) are removed and replaced with mainstream northern Wu readings⁴. The primary purpose of this scheme is for extended Wu-language romanized texts, making Wu-language input and writing faster and more convenient. au [ɔ], iau [iɔ], eu [ɤ], ieu [iɤ] follow Shanghainese; i is realized as [iᶽ]; iu as [y]; ie as [i] with an iq-like rhyme (the exact perception varies among speakers); all other initials, finals, and tones follow Suzhounese. Where Shanghainese and Suzhounese differ in character readings, mainstream Wu dialect readings are consulted to decide the choice (e.g.,“流” is read as lieu2 rather than the Suzhou form leu2 — i.e., Shanghainese ieu-class syllables that merged into eu in Suzhounese are all rendered as ieu;“歳” is read as se5 rather than the Shanghainese form soe5 — i.e., the mainstream Wu form with an e-like rhyme follows the Suzhou reading e). Certain phonetic features distinctive only to Suzhounese or Shanghainese are replaced with mainstream Wu readings. The vocabulary embraces both northern and southern Wu, and permits a wide range of dialectal vocabulary. The grammar, while grounded in northern Wu, draws heavily on southern Wu to restore traditional Wu grammar and reduce Mandarin influence.
